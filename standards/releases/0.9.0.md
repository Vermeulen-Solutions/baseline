# Standards 0.9.0 — Design one section at a time, review by comments, facts entered once

Reviewed 2026-11-26. Standards tag: `standards-v0.9.0`.

What a real site's design review and an EmDash 1.6 upgrade taught, with the runtime unchanged from 0.8.0 (Astro 7.4.12, EmDash 1.6.0; PRs #180–#191).

- **Design one section at a time.** Settle one section with the owner, record what was settled, then carry it to every page type, single templates included, and close each pass with `audit:ui`. On a real club site this beat whole-screen concepts and autonomous critique rounds in the owner's marks.
- **Review by comments.** The owner marks what should change on the design; OpenDesign is the recommended canvas, OpenPencil 0.17 also works.
- **Multilingual sites enter facts once.** Only prose is translatable; `check:i18n-facts` (in `verify`) fails on translated facts.
- **Production content on the local dev server** with `db:local-copy`, including the media and the sandboxed plugins' code.
- **Page changes and pinned scenes:** a card's parts travel as one object, four more page-change styles, no router code on cross-document sites, and a pinned scene holds its last step.

Known gap: on one site taking EmDash 1.6, `emdash media repair-usage --all` (and the MCP `media_usage_repair` with scope `all`) failed with no detail; run per collection, every collection completed. Certification: [README](../../audit/certification-2026-11-18/README.md#standards-090-2026-11-26).

### Changes how pages look: decide per site

#### Page changes: a card's parts travel as one object, and four more styles

_EmDash and static · components, styles, tooling, skills, docs · Copy the named files into the site · files: `src/lib/page-transition-script.ts`, `src/styles/expressive.css`, `scripts/record-motion.mjs`, `scripts/check-art-direction.mjs`_

On sites that set `SiteShell transitionStyle` with cross-document transitions (the starter default):

- **A picked card's parts travel together.** Its photograph, title and any part the site marks (`data-vt-part="date"` in the card, `data-vt-hero-part="date"` on the detail page; `[data-vt-title]` and `[data-vt-hero-title]` for the title) morph into their places on the detail page, and back into the card on history Back or a link marked `data-vt-direction="back"`. Only the picked card is named. A Card's plain `.card-title` is not a part, because a part with no partner on the next page fades on its own. Text parts keep their proportions while they grow, and the held header stays above everything that moves.
- **Four more styles:** `iris` (a circle opening from the point the visitor clicked), `blinds` (six vertical strips), `slide` (the new page slides over the old one from the side) and `stack` (the new page is laid over the old one from below). They are clip paths and transforms on the root snapshots, with reduced motion and the other styles' fallbacks.
- **Arrival works in every browser that runs view transitions:** `[data-arrive]` elements now get their own names (`arrive-1`, `arrive-2` …) from the head script. The stylesheet's generated names (`match-element`) never pair across documents, and Safari ignores them there; they stay for `<ClientRouter />`.
- `record:motion -- --navigate "<link selector>" [--back]` films a page change from a real mouse click, so a review can judge the transition and not only a scroll.
- World-building and the reusable-patterns guide say which style suits which kind of world; the impression tier lists Interlude's other twelve non-WebGL transitions with the world each suits, and moves to `lenis@1.3.28` (a fix for `destroy()` with `autoToggle`).

##### For sites

Copy the listed files; take `SiteShell.astro`'s `TransitionStyle` import and `global.css`'s view-transition rules by hand (the `vt-part` rules and the header group's `z-index`), or through `site:files` where the site has not changed them. To make an entry travel, mark its parts at both ends. A site on `lenis@1.3.27` can stay there; new impression sites install 1.3.28. Look at a page change in a browser with motion allowed after adopting.

#### A pinned scene holds its finished last step before the stage leaves

_EmDash and static · skills, tooling · Copy the named files into the site · files: `scripts/audit-composition.mjs`_

The pinned-scene recipe for the impression tier ended its last step at `contain 90%`, and the variant whose steps replace one another at `contain 100%`. The scene was complete 0.2 of a screen, or not at all, before its stage scrolled away: the visitor never sees it finished. On another project's owner-scored page built that way, the owner's remark was that everything finished opening just as the section left.

- `impression-motion.md`: the example's ranges are now `contain 0% 25%`, `25% 50%`, `50% 70%` on its `300svh` track, and a rule says why and how to work out the hold on other track lengths (`(1 − the last step's end) × (screens − 1)` of at least 0.6).
- `audit:composition` measures it on impression sites: it scrolls through the first pinned scene's range, notes where its scroll-driven animations stop changing, and reports `impression-motion` with "a pinned scene that holds its last step" when less than 0.6 of a screen is left. Scripted (GSAP) scenes are not read.

##### For sites

Impression-tier sites only. Copy `scripts/audit-composition.mjs`; the guide comes with the skill sync. Run `npm run audit:composition` on the page with the scene: if it reports the hold, change the last step's `animation-range` end (by `contain 70%` on a `300svh` track) and move the earlier steps' ranges to match. This changes when the scene's steps arrive, so look at it in a browser with motion allowed.

### Review and merge by hand

#### A replaced image is no longer kept for a year

_EmDash · delivery · Review and merge by hand; a decision per site · files: `src/lib/media-edge-cache.mjs`, `src/middleware.ts`, `src/lib/cloudflare-cache-provider.mjs`, `tests/cloudflare-cache-provider.test.mjs`_

Baseline's middleware and Cloudflare cache provider marked every `/_image` variant and media file `public, max-age=31536000, immutable` and kept an edge copy with no limit. EmDash's Replace original writes new bytes under the same storage key, so a replaced photo could stay old in visitors' browsers for a year; EmDash 1.6 sends `public, max-age=0, must-revalidate` for these files and purges its own route-cache tag, which Baseline's header defeated. Both now go through `src/lib/media-edge-cache.mjs`: the browser receives EmDash's header, the edge copy lives one hour (it still saves the image transform), and media never enters the in-isolate memory cache that EmDash 1.6 asks the route cache to fill.

On Node.js, a site that uses `src/lib/private-safe-memory-cache.mjs` names the provider `memory` in its config (or spreads `memoryCache()`): otherwise EmDash 1.6 keeps images in process memory.

##### For sites

Workers sites that carry Baseline's media caching (look for `immutable` in `src/middleware.ts` or `src/lib/cloudflare-cache-provider.mjs`): copy `src/lib/media-edge-cache.mjs`, then replace the media block in `src/middleware.ts` and the media branch in the provider with the calls to `serveMedia` from Baseline's versions (`site:files` replaces them when the site never edited them). Deploy, then request an image twice and confirm the second answer carries `X-Astro-Cache: HIT` and `Cache-Control: public, max-age=0, must-revalidate`. Images already in visitors' browsers keep the old header until it expires; replaced images show for new visitors within the hour. Node sites with the memory provider: add `name: 'memory'` to the provider entry in `astro.config.mjs`.

#### Cross-document sites no longer ship the client-router script

_EmDash and static · components, delivery · Review and merge by hand; a decision per site_

`SiteShell` wrote about 30 KB of inline client-router script (navigation warming, card morphs, direction, scroll and focus under `<ClientRouter />`) into every page, and every page imported Astro's swap helpers (about 12 KB more). A site with `pageTransitions="cross-document"`, which is the starter default, cannot have a client router, so none of it ran. On the starter's built home page the HTML falls from 48 KB to 17 KB (11.4 KB to 5.2 KB compressed) and the shell's module from 15.7 KB to 3.4 KB.

- The router script is written only when `pageTransitions` is not `cross-document`. A site that adds `<ClientRouter />` already sets `pageTransitions="none"`, as the shell's own comment and the impression-tier recipe say, and keeps everything it had.
- Astro's swap helpers are loaded on demand, only on a page that has `<ClientRouter />`; the router script falls back to its own helpers until they arrive.
- The theme, sticky header, reveal and filter code every page needs is unchanged.

##### For sites

`SiteShell.astro` is Baseline-owned: take it whole when the site has not changed it, otherwise move the router block behind `routerSupport` by hand and make the swap-helper import dynamic. Check one page with `<ClientRouter />` if the site has any: open a page, follow a link and press Back.

### Copy into the site

#### Multilingual sites enter facts once: only prose is translatable (`check:i18n-facts`)

_EmDash · tooling, docs, skills · Copy the named files into the site · files: `scripts/check-i18n-facts.mjs`_

Translations need maintenance, and a site's maintainers do not keep two languages in step. On a real two-language club site every training session, staff member and sponsor was kept twice, and the copies drifted (wrong venues, an outdated coach, times written two ways). The guidance said fields "should simply use `translatable: true`". It now requires the opposite for facts:

- Every field that holds a fact (times, days, dates, numbers, prices, phones, emails, addresses, bank details, links, photos, files, choice lists, switches, order) is created non-translatable.
- A fact that reads differently per language is stored once and written per locale by the code.
- A collection that translates only its title is entered once, in the default locale, with other locales listing it as a fallback; taxonomy terms and slot photos fall back the same way.
- EmDash 1.5 refuses to switch an existing field (`FIELD_UPDATE_REQUIRES_MIGRATION`), so existing sites read the fact from the default locale in code and trash the copies.

`npm run check:i18n-facts` reads the field definitions from the local database. It fails on each translatable field that holds a fact, by type or name, unless `audit/i18n-facts-exceptions.json` gives a reason. It also names collections that translate only their title. Single-language sites, and checkouts without a local database, pass with a note. It is part of `verify` and `verify:fast` on the EmDash profile; the static profiles keep their own `verify` without it.

`docs/internationalization.md` and `emdash-content-modeling` carry the rule; the LinguaDash section is corrected (no side-by-side editing, more providers, translations only on request, content entries only) and positioned for prose.

##### For sites

Copy `scripts/check-i18n-facts.mjs`, add `"check:i18n-facts": "node scripts/check-i18n-facts.mjs"` and put `npm run check:i18n-facts` into `verify` and `verify:fast` after `check:art-direction` (by hand: the site owns `package.json`). On a multilingual site, run it (for a Workers site with `--db` on a `db:local-copy` data.db). Fix each finding by reading the fact from the default locale in code, or record a reason in `audit/i18n-facts-exceptions.json`. The skill sync delivers the content-modeling rule.

#### `db:local-copy`: production content on the local dev server

_EmDash · tooling, docs, skills · Copy the named files into the site · files: `scripts/d1-local-copy.mjs`_

The live site's content could already be exported (`db:export-d1`) and served by `wrangler dev` for an upgrade rehearsal (`db:rehearse`), but nothing fed it to `astro dev`. During a design review on a real site the local database had drifted from production, and the agent did not find the existing export at all, because the tools are named and routed by their first purpose (recovery, upgrade rehearsal). `npm run db:local-copy -- --from <export.sql> --to <dir> --origin <https://live-site>` loads the export into a separate SQLite file, fetches every media file (111 of 153 were missing locally on that site) and the active plugin bundles, and prints the `npm run dev` command that serves it; the site's own `./data.db` and `./uploads` are not touched. The starter's `astro.config.mjs` reads `EMDASH_SQLITE_URL` and `EMDASH_LOCAL_MEDIA_DIR`, falling back to `./data.db` and `./uploads`. `AGENTS.md`'s task table, the command reference and `emdash-site-setup` route "production content on the local dev server" to it.

##### For sites

Copy `scripts/d1-local-copy.mjs` and add the `db:local-copy` script to `package.json`. In `astro.config.mjs`, change the database URL to `process.env.EMDASH_SQLITE_URL || "file:./data.db"` and the local media directory to `process.env.EMDASH_LOCAL_MEDIA_DIR || "./uploads"` (by hand: the site owns that file). Add the `AGENTS.md` task-table row by hand. The skill sync delivers the `emdash-site-setup` pointer.

#### OpenDesign comments show the element's box: check at the owner's width

_EmDash and static · skills, tooling · Copy the named files into the site · files: `scripts/opendesign-comments.mjs`, `tests/opendesign-comments.test.mjs`_

The first comment left by an owner in the OpenDesign app ("too much empty space") sat on empty space, so it attached to `main`. Its stored box was 1955 px wide: the owner's preview was wider than any width the agent checks, and the hero's two columns had drifted to opposite edges there.

`npm run design:comments` now prints each element's box (`box: 1955×784 at 0,81`, and `box` in `--json`). The `opendesign-site-design` review loop explains notes on empty space and asks for before and after renders at the owner's preview width when it differs from 1440 and 390.

##### For sites

Copy the two files; the skill comes with the sync.

#### Review by comments: OpenDesign is the recommended canvas

_EmDash and static · skills, docs, tooling · Copy the named files into the site · files: `scripts/opendesign-comments.mjs`, `tests/opendesign-comments.test.mjs`, `docs/opendesign-onboarding.md`, `docs/commands.md`_

An owner who will not edit a canvas by hand still needs to point at what should change. OpenDesign stores each comment with the element clicked: its `data-od-id`, a selector, its label and its own text. So the agent reads a note, edits that element, checks the render and marks the note resolved, with no matching by position. In Baseline's trial two notes went from reading to resolved, both checked at 1440 and 390, in about thirty seconds of tool time; the same loop on a vector canvas took three to four minutes a note.

- New `npm run design:comments -- --project <id>` lists the open comments with their element; `--resolve <id>` and `--status needs_review <id>` mark them. It finds the running desktop daemon by itself. OpenDesign's MCP server has no comment tools, so the script reads the daemon's local HTTP API, checks the answers' shape, and stops with a clear message if an update changes them.
- The concept checkpoint now recommends OpenDesign when the owner wants to review by marking the design. HTML/browser concepts remain the default without a canvas request; OpenPencil (vector boards, also reviewed by comments), Brilliant (hand editing) and Penpot (shared cloud review) remain explicit choices.
- `opendesign-site-design` gains a "Review by comments" section; the onboarding guide covers the MCP command copied from the app's settings and a review round.

##### For sites

Copy the files above and add the `design:comments` script to `package.json`. The skills and `AGENTS.md` guidance come with the sync.

#### OpenDesign review: the owner's steps, every round

_EmDash and static · skills, docs · Copy the named files into the site · files: `docs/opendesign-onboarding.md`_

In the first review of a real site, the owner had to find the new files without being told where they were (the project's **Design Files** tab) and to know that the desktop window needs `Ctrl + R` after every upload.

`opendesign-site-design` now has the agent give the owner numbered steps in chat every round, with the real project and file names: reload, open the project's Design Files tab, open the named file, comment (not Send to chat), then say done or name the version picked. A pick with no notes is a complete answer. A note on a line inside a box is read against the component it sits in.

For a page that already exists, the first round is a self-contained copy of the live page that the owner marks before any new direction is drawn; CSS animations still run in it.

##### For sites

Copy the onboarding guide; the skill comes with the sync.

#### OpenDesign's reload step names the Mac shortcut

_EmDash and static · skills, docs · Copy the named files into the site · files: `docs/opendesign-onboarding.md`_

The owner's steps for an OpenDesign review said only `Ctrl + R`, the Windows and Linux shortcut. They now say `Ctrl + R` (`Cmd + R` on a Mac) or View › Reload, wherever the reload is mentioned. The Mac shortcut is Electron's standard reload and was not tested on a Mac; the menu works on every system.

Copy the onboarding guide; the skills come with the sync.

#### OpenPencil 0.17: review by comments, and faster boards

_EmDash and static · skills, docs · Copy the named files into the site · files: `docs/openpencil-onboarding.md`_

The `openpencil-site-design` skill now targets OpenPencil 0.17.0, which renamed every MCP tool and changed design JSX, and it is built around the review loop that worked in Baseline's trial: the owner presses C and pins short notes on the boards, and the agent reads each note, finds the layer under it, fixes the cause, replies with what changed and resolves the thread. The owner never edits the canvas by hand.

The skill walks the owner through setup (app, a bridge of the same version, user-scope registration, the two app settings) and writes down what made boards slow: authoring element by element, redrawing an existing HTML page in the canvas and starting a new bridge for every call. Measured tool times are under a second; the skill asks for one JSX render per board, named layers, one export per look and fixes with `replace_id`. OpenPencil's accessibility lint runs before every hand-over. The specimen, the JSX recipe, the scoped-review and connection references and the bundled upstream skill are updated to 0.17.

##### For sites

The skill comes with the skill sync. Copy `docs/openpencil-onboarding.md`. Sites that registered a 0.16 bridge install `@open-pencil/mcp` at the app's version and restart the agent session.

#### The design process's own words on a page are a copy tell

_EmDash and static · skills, tooling · Copy the named files into the site · files: `scripts/copy-tells.mjs`, `scripts/check-quality.mjs`_

A page built from a recorded world can start naming its machinery: a heading that repeats a rule, a caption that calls a photograph "the source object". On another project such a page was marked among the lowest of ten scored rounds, and none of its forty checks read the words.

`check:quality` now reports `copy-tell process word "<phrase>"` for the workflow's two-word phrases in English and French (source object, signature moment, governing idea, pinned scene, named motion, made world, the world's objects, working feature, easy version, first screen; *objet source*, *idée directrice*, *scène épinglée*, *objets du monde*, *premier écran* and a few more). Single words such as "world" or "object" are not read. `copy-voice.md` lists the tell.

##### For sites

Copy `scripts/copy-tells.mjs` and `scripts/check-quality.mjs`; the guide comes with the skill sync. A design studio that uses one of the phrases about its own work adds it to `copyAllow` in `page-budget.json`.

#### Upgrade rehearsals refuse a tracked folder; Kumo counts as a declared import

_EmDash · tooling, dependencies, docs · Copy the named files into the site · files: `scripts/d1-rehearse.mjs`, `scripts/workers-rules.mjs`_

Lessons from taking EmDash 1.6 to five sites.

- `db:rehearse` refuses a `--persist-to` folder Git would track: the copy holds users, sessions and content, and a scratch folder under `.emdash/` was staged for commit on a live site. Keep it under `.emdash/backups/`.
- The rehearsal guide asks for the deploy's own build and environment. A plugin a rehearsal build leaves out is forgotten on start: an email provider included only when a variable was set cleared the site's `email:deliver` selection, which a production build without the variable would have done too.
- The `declared-imports` rule (`audit:site`, `fleet:status`) also reports an undeclared `@cloudflare/kumo`, which EmDash's admin and blocks pin exactly. Baseline declares Kumo 2.19.0 for its `editorial-order` admin page.
- Git Bash rewrites any argument starting with `/` into a Windows path, including URL patterns passed to `node` scripts; set `MSYS_NO_PATHCONV=1`.
- `docs/emdash-overlap.md` lists the Baseline files that overlap EmDash behaviour, with what to re-check on each EmDash upgrade; a test keeps it complete.

##### For sites

Copy `scripts/d1-rehearse.mjs` and `scripts/workers-rules.mjs` (or run them from a Baseline checkout). A site whose own plugins import `@cloudflare/kumo` declares it at the version its EmDash admin pins (2.19.0 with EmDash 1.6).

### Arrives with the site's sync

#### Design by settling one section with the owner, then carrying it across the site

_EmDash and static · skills, docs · Arrives with the site's skill or source sync_

On a real club site the step that held was settling one section's styling with the owner, then carrying it to the rest of the site one section at a time: the first section was marked 7.5 and the pages after it about 7, from 6.5, where whole-screen concepts had stalled at about 6.5 over fourteen rounds of trials. One careful pass beat four autonomous critique rounds on the same section (7.5 against 6.5). The rest of the owner's time went on gaps this procedure now closes: wrong facts designed well, a settled treatment replaced later, enlarged text unchecked, and single templates left out.

- `site-art-direction/references/design-iteration.md` gains "Settle one section, then carry it across the site": pick the seed section, check its facts with the owner before designing it, make one considered pass checked for defects only and let the owner mark it, record what was settled, carry it to every page type (single templates included) one section at a time, and close each pass with `audit:ui`.
- The Art Direction Record schema gains an optional `Settled Treatments` field (`site-art-direction`); a different treatment for a settled component needs the owner's say first.
- `astro-site-design/references/accessibility.md`: run `audit:ui`'s enlarged-text cases on every touched page type, stack cards with container queries in `rem`, and the zero-width collapse of `container-type: inline-size` on a box its parent sizes by content.
- `emdash-site-setup`: what changing content on a live EmDash 1.5.0 site needs (token refresh, `_rev`, links through the content API and publishing the staged draft, roles through `taxonomies`, the failing translation of linked entries, the page cache) and how to log the CLI in to a local dev server whose database is a copy of a deployed site.

##### For sites

The skill sync delivers the skill changes. In the site's `AGENTS.md`, add the "Roll out and redesign by settling one section…" sentence to the design iteration section by hand (the site owns that file), and add `Settled Treatments` to the Art Direction Record when the owner settles a treatment.
