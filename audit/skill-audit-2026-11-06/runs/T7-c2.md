## Answer

Context: a new five-route, single-locale English greenfield site, so the Express track applies (at most six routes, one locale). This is Stage 4, art direction. No visual implementation happens before the saved `## Art Direction Record` passes `npm run check:art-direction -- --require-record`. The profile is not yet chosen; that is a Stage 2 decision, and a static profile is the likely fit for five marketing pages. Nothing below depends on it.

### Working assumptions (labelled, reversible)

- No canvas was requested, so concept work uses **HTML/browser concepts** under `audit/visual-concepts/<round>/`: one file per direction plus one comparison `index.html`. I would name Brilliant, OpenPencil and Penpot once at the concept checkpoint, without making the answer a blocking stop.
- Compare two or three structurally distinct directions (different hero idea, proof treatment and section composition), not colour skins, and select one before broad implementation. Run the concept quality gate table, and the reference-research step with its single inspiration offer if the owner has supplied no references.
- Fixed commitments: the logo and the brand blue are used faithfully. Content limits: three publishable customer quotes, product screenshots, and no usage statistics.
- Pricing facts (plans, prices, trial terms, any guarantee) were not supplied. They must come from the owner, and I would ask before composing the pricing page. Until then pricing shows clearly marked placeholders whose shape matches the real content.

### Aesthetic lane

**Product-Led Minimalism.** The offer is a focused productivity app for freelancers. The lane covers modern SaaS and productivity apps and is built around one restrained accent, which here is the supplied brand blue.

- **Canvas and surfaces:** optical-white canvas, flat planes with crisp 1px borders, and the three surface tiers (`--color-page`, `--color-surface`, elevated).
- **Corners and density:** 6-10px radius, standard spatial density.
- **Accent:** brand blue is the only action and accent colour. I would verify its contrast on light and dark surfaces with `npm run design:tokens`.
- **Narrative metaphor (concrete, bounded):** a freelancer's ruled timesheet. It shows up as hairline row rules in the product specimen and the pricing comparison matrix, and stays absent from the logo, icons and form controls.
- **Why not the others:** Technical Systems implies a developer audience and a dark telemetry look. Luminous Futurism is the wrong tone for solo freelancers. Editorial Paper has no editorial voice to carry it.
- **Expressive range:** composition varies where persuasion happens (home) and stays orderly on pricing, where visitors compare. Motion is quiet (feedback and reveals), with reduced motion respected. Density is standard.

### Header and navigation archetype

**Floating Glass Capsule** (`.surface-glass`), the default pairing for Product-Led Minimalism.

- Five top-level links (Home, Features, Pricing, About, Contact) fit a capsule, so there is no recorded navigation need to deviate.
- One primary action button sits in the capsule, with the same label used across the site.
- Keep real navigation semantics, a skip link and a mobile menu.
- Glass navigation is on the generic-fingerprint list, so treat it as a lifted, mostly opaque capsule without stacked blur effects, and re-check it in the fingerprint review.

### Hero visual strategy

**Live-coded UI specimen** (`.ui-specimen`), because the offer is software (first-match rule b).

- Build an HTML/CSS/SVG recreation of the product's core view (a running timer, a few time-entry rows, a weekly summary), based on the supplied screenshots so it stays faithful to the app.
- The skill says not to use blurry raster screenshots in the hero. The supplied screenshots stay in use on the Features page as sharp framed figures, each with a source width of at least its rendered width at 2x, a caption and meaningful alt text.
- Values in the specimen are sample data, labelled "Sample data". They are never presented as customer results or statistics, since the company has none to publish.
- No stock imagery and no generic futuristic art. The heading carries itself, with no kicker above the `<h1>`.
- If the owner fixes a different hero treatment, record that exception and the reason.

### Typography voices and labels

- **Display (required):** a modern grotesque (Geist, Inter Display or Plus Jakarta Sans class) with tight tracking (-0.02em to -0.03em), compressed line-height and `text-wrap: balance`. The final pick comes from fonts the project can legally and reliably serve.
- **Body (required):** a neutral high-x-height sans (the same family is fine), 1.6-1.68 line-height, 60-68ch measure.
- **Inline editorial italic accent: not used.** The brand has no editorial voice, and an italic word in each headline is a generated-site fingerprint.
- **Mono / telemetry: used narrowly.** It is reserved for real data in the specimen and on pricing (timer values, durations, dates, prices), with `.tabular-nums`. So the site uses three voices in effect: display, body, and a small mono for data.
- **Labels:** no kicker labels above headings, no numbered section indices and no `[01 // ...]` coordinates. The one exception is a numbered "how it works" sequence, because that order carries meaning. A plan badge such as "Most popular" appears only if a real plan warrants it.
- The logo wordmark is used as supplied and is not retyped.

### Home page section sequence (primary action: start using the app; terms await the owner)

1. **Hero:** one `<h1>` using an `{Outcome} without {Pain}` formula in the freelancer's vocabulary, a one or two sentence subheadline, one primary CTA with a concrete label, and the live-coded specimen. Show a risk-reversal line only if real trial or cancellation terms exist.
2. **Proof band, using the three real quotes:** an asymmetric quote band (one large, two smaller, attributed as supplied, with permission confirmed). It is not a metric ledger, because there are no statistics, and not a logo ticker, because no logo permissions were mentioned.
3. **Problem to solution:** the friction of manual or spreadsheet tracking against the app's model, as a split layout with prose on one side and a sharp product figure on the other.
4. **Feature and capability variance:** an asymmetric `.bento-grid` (8/4) of three or four capabilities, each pairing a bold outcome with its mechanism and optionally a real screenshot crop. The geometry differs from the sections around it.
5. **How it works:** a numbered three-step sequence on a hairline ledger. The numbers are earned because the steps are an actual order.
6. **Objections and FAQ:** native `<Disclosure>` covering real objections (price, data export, devices, switching), with matching `FAQPage` JSON-LD. Answers use only facts the owner has confirmed.
7. **Closing conversion band:** `data-surface="dark"` spotlight repeating the identical primary CTA.
8. **Footer:** semantic landmarks, privacy and terms links.

Pacing: no two equal 3-column card grids in a row, and do not copy the default bento, metric strip, dark band and FAQ rhythm onto every page.

### Pricing page section sequence (a comparison task, so composition stays orderly)

1. **Intro and plan cards:** a short `<h1>`, then equal-weight plan cards in one row. Prices and billing period use tabular mono. A monthly/annual toggle appears only if both options really exist. One primary CTA per plan, in the same label vocabulary as the rest of the site.
2. **Comparison matrix:** a `.grid-hairline` ledger comparing plans row by row. This is where the ruled-timesheet metaphor shows.
3. **One real quote** that speaks to value or ease of switching, from the three supplied.
4. **Objection FAQ:** billing, cancellation, taxes, invoices, switching, with `FAQPage` JSON-LD.
5. **Closing band:** the repeated primary CTA, plus a link to Contact for questions.

Do not invent a money-back guarantee, free trial, discounts, customer counts or "trusted by" claims. If the owner supplies terms, place them beside the CTA.

### Site anti-patterns (each with its reason)

1. **No invented numbers, user counts, ratings or "trusted by" strips.** The company has no publishable statistics, and the skill treats fabricated proof as a defect. A missing metric calls for an honest alternative, here three real quotes and a labelled sample specimen.
2. **No kicker labels, numbered section indices, italic serif accent words or mono "terminal" decoration.** The brand has no code, telemetry or editorial identity, and these read as generated-site fingerprints. Mono stays on real time and price data.
3. **No blurry or shrunken raster screenshots as the hero and no stock "happy freelancer at a laptop" imagery.** The hero must show the real thing in a sharp rebuild, and stock art would make the site interchangeable with any other.
4. **No identical card rows, or one section recipe repeated across pages.** Varied geometry keeps the home page persuasive. The exception is pricing, where uniform plan cards are justified because visitors compare.
5. **No neon glows, gradient washes, pill-everything buttons or busy decorative badges.** They conflict with the lane's quiet single-accent clarity and would dilute the brand blue as the action colour.

### Record to save in AGENTS.md before visual work

- Aesthetic Lane: Product-Led Minimalism (brand blue as the single accent).
- Narrative Metaphor: freelancer's ruled timesheet, with hairline rows in the specimen and pricing matrix, absent from logo, icons and controls.
- Typography Pairing: modern grotesque display, neutral sans body, tabular mono for real data only.
- Token Physics: optical-white canvas; brand blue as action and accent; 1px hairline borders with 6-10px radius; standard spatial density.
- Site Anti-Patterns: two or three of the bans above, semicolon-separated.

Then run `npm run design:tokens` and the `/_styleguide` calibration (Stage 4.5) before page assembly.
