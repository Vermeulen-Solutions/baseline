## Answer

Proposal only. Nothing is implemented. The decisions are meant to go into the `## Art Direction Record` in AGENTS.md. `npm run check:art-direction -- --require-record` then has to pass before any visual work.

### Process framing

- **Track and profile.** Five routes, one locale (English), no integrations. That is Express (new-site-workflow SKILL, "Choose the track"; express-track.md). Express still has a direction comparison and all the CLI gates.
  - Platform: recommend `static`, labelled as an assumption. Ask about editing needs before choosing a CMS profile.
- **Design surface.** No canvas has been mentioned, so the default is HTML/browser concepts (AGENTS.md; design-iteration.md).
  - Mention Brilliant, OpenPencil and Penpot once, without making it a blocking question.
  - Compare two or three structurally distinct directions side by side in `audit/visual-concepts/<round>/`. Differences should be in hero idea, proof placement and section composition, not just colour.
  - Recommend one direction and pass the concept quality gate table.
- **Reference research.** Run the one inspiration offer. Reuse any references the company supplies.
- **Spacing.** Shared layout spacing defaults, standard density.
- **Brand assets.** Preserve the supplied logo master. Extract a symbol for the favicon rather than shrinking the lockup. Check it at 16/24/32px, and as a one-colour version and on a reversed ground (brand-assets.md).
- **Brand blue.** Use it as the single accent seed (`design:tokens --seed-accent`). Verify the WCAG contrast pairs, especially blue text on white and white text on blue buttons. Adjust the tint if a pair fails.

### Aesthetic lane

**Product-Led Minimalism**, with the brand blue as the restrained cobalt-type accent.

- Why: the offer is a focused productivity tool for solo professionals. This is the lane's stated fit ("modern SaaS, productivity apps, creator tools"). The brand colour is already a single blue.
- Technical Systems was rejected. It would make a tool for freelancers feel like infrastructure and push the dark, dense, mono look.
- Luminous Futurism was rejected. It carries AI and gaming connotations and a dark glass look that does not suit a calm, trust-first product.
- Editorial Paper was rejected. There is no editorial voice to carry.

**Narrative metaphor:** a drafting table or ruled timesheet. It means precise, ruled, quiet work.
- Where it shows: hairline rules, tabular alignment of durations and rates, a calm paper-white canvas, and screenshots framed like sheets laid on the table.
- Where it stays absent: no literal paper textures, no clock or hourglass clip-art, and nothing that overrides the logo or product UI.

**Expressive range**
- Composition: low to moderate. Pricing and comparison pages need scanning speed. Spend variation on the home page.
- Motion: quiet. A small state change in the hero specimen and subtle reveals, with full reduced-motion behaviour.
- Density: standard.

**Token physics**
- Canvas: optical white, with 3-tier surfaces (page, surface, elevated).
- Accent: brand blue for primary actions only.
- Borders: 1px hairlines, 6-10px radius.
- Dark surface: used once, for the closing CTA band.

### Header and navigation archetype

**Floating capsule geometry on a solid lifted surface:** `.surface-elevated` with a 1px border and no backdrop blur.
- This is the lane's default pairing. Glass is a generated-site tell on a flat-plane lane.
- Five top-level items (Home, Features, Pricing, About, Contact) fit a capsule, so no navigation need justifies departing from the default.
- Logo on the left. One primary CTA button on the right, e.g. a "Start …" action. The label depends on terms the client has to confirm (see Open questions). Use the same label on every page.
- Contact stays a normal nav link.
- Keep real nav semantics, `aria-current`, and a mobile disclosure that works with a keyboard.

### Hero visual strategy

**Choice: strategy 1, a live-coded UI specimen (`.ui-specimen`).** The offer is software, which is the first matching rule in site-art-direction.

- Build the specimen in HTML/CSS as a real timer panel: running timer, a short entries list, project and rate labels. Use `.tabular-nums` for durations and rates.
- Use the supplied product screenshots as a reference, so the specimen is faithful to the real UI. Do not hand-wave a different interface.
- All content in the specimen must be clearly sample or illustrative data. Show no usage numbers, user counts or earnings claims. The company has no statistics to publish.
- The supplied screenshots go into feature sections, prepared with `npm run media:prepare`. Use them at native resolution, in hairline frames, with captions that say what is shown. The skill forbids blurry raster screenshots as the hero specimen. They are most useful where their detail is legible.
- Fallback: if the specimen cannot be made faithful at reasonable effort, use one framed, sharp screenshot as a Custom treatment and record that exception and why.
- Rejected: stock photos of freelancers at laptops, and abstract gradient art. Neither shows the product.

### Home page section sequence

Proof is thin: three short quotes, no statistics, and no logos mentioned. So the order follows the argument and omits unsupported strips.

1. **Hero.**
   - One `<h1>` that states the outcome (formula "{Outcome} without {Pain}"). Draft for the client to confirm; no invented claims.
   - One or two sentences of subhead, one primary CTA, and the specimen.
   - A risk reversal next to the CTA only if the client confirms real terms (trial, no card, cancel anytime).
2. **One lead customer quote** directly after the hero. It answers "does it work for people like me". Attribute it exactly as supplied and as they permit.
3. **Problem to solution.** A short contrast: what freelancers do now (spreadsheets, forgetting timers, reconstructing hours for invoices) against what the app does. Keep it to what the client confirms.
4. **How it works.** Three steps, numbered because the sequence is real. Do this only if the real flow is a sequence; otherwise use plain labels. Pair each step with a framed screenshot.
5. **Feature variance.** One asymmetric split (7/5) with a framed screenshot, then a different geometry such as a hairline matrix or sticky sidebar list for the remaining capabilities. Link to /features. Do not stack identical 3-column card rows.
6. **The remaining two quotes.** Place them as a paired block that answers objections (ease of setup, trust). They should not just repeat section 2.
7. **Pricing teaser.** A short summary pointing to /pricing, with real figures only if the client supplies them.
8. **Closing CTA band.** One inverted `data-surface="dark"` band with the same primary action label as the hero.

- Omitted on purpose: metric strip, logo ticker, home FAQ (it lives on /pricing), and the default bento then metrics then dark band then FAQ sequence.

### Pricing page section sequence

Archetype: Subscription/Sales, which is a comparison and decision task. Composition stays orderly, and uniform rows are justified here.

1. **Compact page opener.** Heading and one line of context. No specimen or large hero. Include a billing toggle only if monthly and annual both exist.
2. **Plan cards.** Real tiers and prices from the client, with prices in tabular numerals. Mark a "recommended" plan only if the client states one. The primary action is identical on every card or consistently paired with the plan. Keep card geometry uniform.
3. **Comparison matrix.** A hairline-ruled table of what each plan includes. Skip it if there is only one plan or the differences fit in the cards.
4. **One quote** that speaks to value or ease. Do not reuse the one placed directly after the home hero.
5. **FAQ.** Native `<Disclosure>` (details) accordion, ordered by objection severity, with `FAQPage` JSON-LD. Billing, cancellation, data export and invoices. Use only answers the client confirms.
6. **Closing line and CTA,** or a pointer to Contact for questions. Lighter than on the home page.

- Omit any guarantee, "no credit card" or money-back claim unless the client supplies approved terms.
- Omit fabricated customer counts, ratings or savings.

### Typography voices and labels

Use three voices at most. A two-voice system is valid, and the mono voice is only used where the content earns it.

| Voice | Use | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Display (`--font-display`) | Headings | A modern grotesque such as Geist or Inter Display. Tracking about -0.02 to -0.03em for this lane. Balanced wrapping. Check descenders at tight leading. |
| Body (`--font-body`) | Reading and UI copy | Inter or Geist. Line-height about 1.6, measure 60-68ch. |
| Mono (`--font-mono` with `.tabular-nums`) | **Earned, narrowly** | Timer readouts and durations in the specimen, rates, and prices. This is real data. |
| Italic serif accent (`.font-accent`) | **Not used** | No editorial voice in the brand. An italic word per headline is a generated-site tell. |

- Fonts must be licensed, served reliably, and cover the weights used. Verify this in implementation.
- **Labels:** plain, sentence-case headings. No kicker labels above headings, no mono eyebrow labels, and no numbered section indices.
  - Numbers appear only where order is real (the three "how it works" steps, if applicable).
  - Pills and badges only for real metadata (for example a plan name or a real "new" feature).
- **Calls to action:** an active verb plus a concrete outcome. Never "Submit", "Get started" or "Learn more". The same primary label on hero, mid-page and closing band.

### Site anti-patterns, with reasons

1. **No invented proof.** No counters, user numbers, star ratings, "trusted by" logos, or testimonials beyond the three supplied quotes, and no stat-style claims.
   - Reason: the company has nothing it wants to publish. The skill says missing proof means an honest alternative or an omitted section, never fabricated evidence.
2. **No generated-SaaS fingerprints.** No kicker or mono labels above headings, numbered section indices, italic serif accent words, gradient text, glass or blurred navigation, or purple-blue glow backgrounds.
   - Reason: the generic-fingerprint check in astro-site-design says these read as generated when used by default, and glass on a flat-plane lane contradicts its surface physics.
3. **No stock-photo or blurry-screenshot hero; no repeated identical card rows.** No generic freelancer-at-laptop stock imagery. No upscaled or low-resolution screenshots as hero art. No back-to-back identical 3-column card grids, and no same-sequence-on-every-page layout.
   - Reason: the hero must show the real product. Variation in section geometry is the rubric's grid-rhythm requirement, and a copy-paste sequence is itself a generic tell.

For the Art Direction Record, use the first three as the semicolon-separated Site Anti-Patterns.

### Open questions (for the client, or labelled working assumptions)

- What exactly is the primary action (free trial, sign-up, demo), and what terms can be stated next to it?
- Plan names, prices, billing periods and what is in each plan.
- Whether the quote authors' names, roles and logos may be published as supplied. Confirm they are happy with the exact wording.
- Whether the product flow really is three steps.
- Editing needs (static files versus a CMS) and a contact form handler for /contact.
