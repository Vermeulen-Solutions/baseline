# Optional visitor research

Read for a requested usability/findability study, participant interview plan or synthesis of supplied visitor evidence. Use it when an important audience/task/navigation uncertainty merits research; ordinary sites with a clear brief do not need a new interview, service or mandatory research stage. Reuse discovery, page briefs and existing evidence.

## Plan one useful decision

State what decision the study should inform and what existing evidence cannot resolve. Choose the lightest appropriate method: a first-click/tree task for navigation, a usability task on the real page/prototype, an interview for experience or needs, or synthesis of existing notes. Recruiting, contacting participants, recording and external research services need their own applicable scope; writing a plan does not imply those actions happened.

Name the relevant visitor criteria, artifact/version, neutral task, observable success and what to note. For example: “You want to ask whether the service covers your town. Show where you would start.” Do not reveal the intended menu label in the task. Capture the participant's route, interpretation, hesitation, outcome and relevant quote, including disagreement. Run a small pilot if collecting new evidence; choose task/participant counts for the decision instead of importing fixed catalogue quotas. Keep personal data and raw research in the agreed private home.

## Synthesize with traceability

Assign stable participant/source IDs and source locations to observations. Distinguish what was said/done from the analyst's interpretation and proposed change. Group observations into themes without dropping contradictory evidence. Count unique participants against the stated eligible sample; repeated quotes or sessions are not additional people. Exclude withdrawn/out-of-scope material and state missing evidence. Do not turn a small qualitative sample into population percentages or a commercial-performance claim.

For each recommendation retain: evidence IDs/locations → observed issue → interpretation/confidence → proposed action → what would verify the change. Quote exactly when quoting. If only owner feedback, agent critique or synthetic fixtures are available, label them accordingly: they may rehearse a process but do not establish observed visitor behavior. Do not invent participants, quotations or recruitment. Separate actual findings from hypotheses worth testing.

Return a concise decision-ready summary with scope/sample, themes, counterevidence, prioritized recommendations and unanswered questions. Reuse the site's existing notes; no additional product-context system is required. Implement only the research-related changes already authorized, preserving content/identity commitments. A hypothetical task script or synthetic rehearsal should remain explicitly unexecuted as a real study.
