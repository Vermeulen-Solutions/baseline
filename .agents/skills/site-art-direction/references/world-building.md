# Building the site's world

Read after [distinctive direction](distinctive-direction.md) names the governing idea, and before concepts. The governing idea says what the site is; this step builds it. Required for every new site and new direction; the Art Direction Record's `World` and `Decisions` fields hold the result, and `check:art-direction` checks them with the reference research.

## Why

Measured on their home pages, sites built by small independent studios for a race, an embroidery shop and a school render 650 to 1,200 elements, three type voices in 20 or more sizes with a display line up to eight times the body size, 30 to 45 colours, 13 to 120 drawn SVG objects, rotated and shadowed pieces, and 7 to 80 running animations. Three trial builds from Baseline rendered 150 elements, one or two voices in 9 sizes, 11 colours, no drawn object, no rotation and no animation. The trials were tidy and correct and looked like templates: every section was a heading, a paragraph, a photograph and a ruled list.

The studio sites are made of **things from the subject's own world**, and each world is made of something different: one of terrain, weather and timing, one of cloth and thread, one of a printed catalogue. Every section is a thing, dressed in its material, and every thing comes from the client's trade. That is the level this step aims at.

**Every device has a source.** An earlier version of this page listed ready devices, and twenty-one trial sites for unrelated trades (a plumber, a restaurant, a night club, a sculptor, a village association) came out with the same ones: a paper ground, a condensed display face, labels in a mono face, a stamp set at an angle and a running band of words. None of those sites was wrong, and together they were one site. This page lists no devices. What the site is made of comes from two places you can point at: the client's own material (their message and their photographs) and pictures of the trade's real things, found and saved in step 1. A device you cannot trace to one of those is a habit; leave it out.

## 1. Gather the world (never wait for the owner)

Do these in order. They take about fifteen minutes and decide more of the result than any later step.

1. **Read the client's photographs.** Open every photograph and write one line for each in `audit/world/notes.md`: what it shows, what the things in it are made of, any lettering in it and how that lettering is made (painted, cut, cast, lit, printed, chalked, sewn), and how the light falls.
2. **Read their colours.** Run `npm run design:world -- palette <the folder of photographs>`. It prints the large colours of the photographs and the small vivid ones. The palette is built from these values in step 6.
3. **Find the trade's real things.** Run `npm run design:world -- search "<a thing>"` for at least four different things this trade makes, uses, wears, drives, hangs up or hands over, in English and in the client's language. Search for the thing itself (a tool, a product, a sign, a place, a garment, a vehicle, a document), not for an adjective or a mood. Save at least six pictures with `npm run design:world -- get <numbers>`; they go to `audit/world/` with their source and licence in `audit/world/sources.md`. Search for the thing as it is today: an archive holds many old prints, and an old engraving on the site of a present-day trade is a costume, right only when the client's own identity is historical. **At most one of your searches is for printed matter** (a ticket, a label, a poster, a card, a packet, a form). The others are for objects, places, materials, tools and machines. An archive is full of printed paper, and an agent that goes looking for tickets and labels finds them for every trade: that is how one paper look came back into trial sites whose records were otherwise taken from pictures. In the  line, at most one of the saved pictures is printed matter.
4. **Look at each saved picture** and add its line to `audit/world/notes.md`, as for the photographs: the materials, the lettering and how it is made, the colours, how the thing is put together, how it moves or is used.
5. **Write the inventory:** at least **twelve** things, each seen in a photograph, in a saved picture or named in the client's message, in five kinds:

   | Kind | Ask |
   | --- | --- |
   | Objects | What does the trade make, use, hand over, hang up, wear? |
   | Documents and signs | What does it write, display, mark, post or light up? |
   | Surfaces | What are its materials and grounds, and what do they look like close up? |
   | Marks | What does it leave behind, measure, number or sign? |
   | Motion | What moves there, and how? |

6. **Name what the world is made of:** two or three materials a visitor would see or touch there, each with the picture that shows it. Paper and print are one answer among many (metal, water, glass, light and darkness, cloth, clay, wood, stone, enamel, a screen, earth), and the right one only when the photographs and saved pictures show paper and print.
7. **Choose the source object:** the one thing the whole site is built like, named in the governing idea, and visible in a photograph or a saved picture. Then set the palette from the colours of step 2: four to six colours with roles (a ground, an ink, one or two materials, an action colour, a mark colour). The ground is a large colour of the photographs, not a default.

The owner's own things come first when they exist (their sign, logo, van, card, packaging, the labels on their shelves). Never ask the owner to supply this: it is design work, and a real owner rarely has an answer. Facts are different: dates, prices, names and numbers come from the owner or stay marked placeholders. A picture from the archive is shown on the site only when its line in `sources.md` says it may be, with the credit that line asks for; every picture may be looked at and learned from.

## 2. Build the kit

Design these before any page, in the concept, and record them in `World`. For each one, the answer comes from the notes of step 1: if the notes do not hold it, go back and look again rather than inventing it.

1. **Objects, one per section, on every page.** Each section of every page (the about page and the contact page too, not only the home page) is set as one thing from the inventory, built the way that thing is built. A bare section (heading, paragraph, photograph) is allowed once per page, for a story that needs only words. An object carries the trade's real fields as the thing itself carries them, in the thing's own lettering. Small capitals, a mono face, middle dots and arrows are neither good nor bad in themselves. They read as a generated page when they are chrome laid over every section of any subject: the same tracked label above every heading, a meta line of dots under every title, an arrow on every link.
2. **Surfaces, two or three.** Grounds from the materials named in step 1, drawn as those materials look in the pictures (their grain, sheen, joints, wear), and edges between grounds that those materials have. A flat colour band between sections is the template's edge.
3. **Type in two voices, and a third only when the world has one.** Choose each from how this world letters its own things, as the notes describe it. A display face whose construction matches that lettering, with a treatment that is how the lettering is made, and a reading face. Add a third voice for labels, codes and numbers only when a picture shows that the world letters its small things in another way (a typed form, a stamped number, a chalked price), and then take it from that picture. Pages that professional designers preferred, on a set of 95 ranked by their votes, used two voices as often as three; a third voice added because a rule asked for one is how a mono label ended up over every heading of the trial sites. **A section heading stands alone:** no small label above it that repeats what it says. On those 95 pages no designer-made page had such labels on three sections, and every one of the best-ranked generated pages did. **The page heading is set in one style:** no single word in italics for emphasis, which a third of the best generated pages had and no designer-made page. Choose the display face by looking, never from memory: every trial site named a face it already knew (three club sites the same one, three restaurants the same one) and wrote the reason afterwards. Say in one word how the lettering in the pictures is built, then run `npm run design:world -- faces <that kind> --text "<the site's display line>"` (the kinds: didone, old-style, slab, grotesque, geometric, humanist, condensed, stencil, brush, script, hand, inscribed, lights, poster, typewriter, blackletter, rounded). It makes a specimen sheet of eight open faces of that kind with the site's own line, `audit/world/faces-1.png`, and records them in `audit/world/faces.md`. Open the sheet beside the lettering picture and take the closest; run it again for another sheet or another kind when none fits; add a face you have in mind with `--include "<family>"` so it is judged beside the others. `check:art-direction` checks that the record's display face is on a sheet. Each face installs as an `npm` package ([Fontsource](https://fontsource.org/)); more open faces are at [Fontshare](https://www.fontshare.com/), [Use & Modify](https://usemodify.com/) and [Uncut](https://uncut.wtf/). Check the accented characters of the site's language on the sheet. Use the whole scale: labels at 11 to 13px up to a display line at six to eight times the body size on a desktop, once or twice a page (past the craft floor's 6rem cap, which the recorded World overrides; cap it at `17vi` so it fits a 320px screen with the text enlarged). Keep the other headings at half the display line or less, so it reads as the page's statement: `audit:composition` looks for an opening line at least twice the median heading.
4. **Depth and physics.** Objects behave as their materials do: what hangs swings a little, what is stacked overlaps, what is lit glows and throws light, what is heavy sits flat. One light direction for every shadow. Rotation and shadow are an object's physics, never a style applied to everything.
5. **Photographs framed by the world, never stretched.** Photographs go into frames taken from the inventory, with one tone treatment (`data-tone`), so they read as a designed set. A photograph is never shown wider than its own pixels: an owner's phone photograph 600 to 1,500px wide sits in an object at that width or smaller, or is cropped to a detail, and the first screen is carried by an object or a generated artefact from the world with the photograph inside it. A trial home page opened on a 1,024px photograph stretched to 1,440px, and every reviewer named it as the ceiling of the design. Full width is for a photograph whose file is at least as wide as the screen it fills; prepare it with `media:prepare -- --max-edge 2880` so a dense screen gets enough pixels (the default edge is 2400). Look at what each photograph shows before placing or captioning it: a supplied or stand-in picture can show the wrong country, trade or season (a trial's stand-in of a leak repair carried a sign in another language, which only a reviewer noticed).
6. **Generated artefacts where photographs are missing.** Make one per item from its data, in a form the inventory holds. A geometric glyph system in the site's palette is better than a stock photograph.

   **Drawings: code draws geometry; figures come from a drawn set or a photograph.** Written as SVG coordinates, an agent draws circles, rules, gauges, schematics, maps, routes, rings and patterns well, and animals, food, plants, people and tools badly: it cannot see the curve it writes, and every trial's hand-drawn owl, marmot, bicycle or cheese came out as clip-art beside professional photographs. So code the geometric objects; take a figurative subject from one open-licence drawn set, `npm run design:icons -- search owl` then `npm run design:icons -- get game-icons:owl --out src/assets/drawn` (search lists figurative sets first and marks UI and emoji sets, which read as an interface or a chat; the command refuses sets that are not open, writes the credit, and warns when a second set joins the first), or show it in a photograph (cut out, framed, toned) or in an archive picture whose licence allows it. One set per site, so every drawing has one hand. Recolouring alone leaves a set figure a logo: give each figure the medium this world marks its things with, as a filter and a mask, show it at least 480px on its short side, and set it on the object it marks; below about 300px it stays an icon whatever the treatment ([drawing treatments](drawing-treatments.md) has the techniques). A set under CC BY needs a credit visitors can see (a credits line in the footer). Emoji sets read as chat, not as a made world; leave them for sites whose world is playful. When no set has the subject, use the photograph or leave the subject to the words: a crude drawing lowers the page more than a missing one. Judge every drawing at full size before keeping it.
7. **Motion from the world, in four layers.** The studio sites run 70 to 140 CSS animations on a home page, with no animation library, and the page feels alive because its motion is how the subject's own things move. Take each motion from the Motion row of the inventory: describe how the thing moves in the real world, then build that.
   - *One arrival:* the first screen arrives as a short orchestrated sequence of the world's own motions, in at most three stages and under a second.
   - *World motions:* three to five named motions, each on the object it belongs to, bringing the objects in as the visitor scrolls. A shared quiet entrance (`data-reveal`) can carry the plain text and lists between them, as it does on the studio sites. Each world motion is a CSS animation whose `@keyframes` name is the name in the record, so the audit can see it run; a trial recorded four motions and built two.
   - *Ambient:* one or two slow loops of something that really keeps moving in this world, 8 seconds or longer per cycle, on a decorative layer, paused on hover and focus.
   - *Feedback:* motion that answers the visitor (opening, choosing, confirming) shows what changed, and the feature's own state changes carry most of it ([interaction motion](../../astro-site-design/references/interaction-motion.md)).

   Two more carry the world across the site. *Between pages:* set `SiteShell transitionStyle` (fade, lift, curtain, wipe) or a page's `data-arrive` to the one closest to how this world changes scene, so a page change is part of the world, not a cut; trials that built rich pages left every page change a plain cut. *The scroll itself:* when the site's job is the impression and its sections are staged on the scroll, smooth the wheel scroll ([smooth scroll](../../astro-site-design/references/smooth-scroll.md)); when the visitor comes to do something, leave it native.

   Everything holds still under `prefers-reduced-motion`, with the final state shown, and text the visitor is reading never moves once it has arrived.
8. **One working feature from the client's own material that does the site's job.** A tool, not a decoration: something the visitor uses to do what they came for, built from the client's real data in a form the inventory holds. Infer it from the job and the trade; build it with real behaviour and an honest fallback.

## 3. Write the decisions down

For the look, the signature feature and the content, write the **easy version** (what any agent or template would do for this trade) and **what this site does instead**, at least three entries. The easy version for a craft is a dark or cream ground, one earthy accent, a soft serif and full-width photographs; for any site, the current defaults of generated pages: a warm cream ground with a serif display and a terracotta accent, near-black with one acid accent, a broadsheet of hairline rules, identical rounded cards with one soft shadow; for a school, a hero photograph of an empty classroom and course cards; for an event, black and a loud orange. Baseline's own easy version is the look described under "Why": the paper ground, the condensed display face, the mono labels, the angled stamp and the running band of words, on whatever trade. A direction whose look is an easy version is not finished, however well it is built.

## 4. Techniques

Build rules that hold whatever the world is. Decorative layers are `aria-hidden="true"`, add no layout shift (reserve their space), and keep text contrast against the actual ground.

- **A textured ground:** a small tiling texture (a 256px WebP or an inline SVG pattern of a few hundred bytes) as `background-image`, at low contrast; set `background-color` separately so a later shorthand does not wipe it. A detail cropped from one of the client's photographs or from a saved picture whose licence allows it beats a generated noise filter. Free CC0 material textures: [Poly Haven](https://polyhaven.com/textures) and [ambientCG](https://ambientcg.com/).
- **A shaped edge between two grounds:** an SVG path as a `mask-image` or a pseudo-element at the section's top edge, in the next section's colour. Shapes cut into an object (a hole, a notch, a window): a `radial-gradient` or SVG mask.
- **Lettering treatments:** `text-shadow` for a second colour or a glow, `-webkit-text-stroke` for an outline, a mask for uneven coverage, `mix-blend-mode` for lettering that sits in its ground. Choose the one that is how this world's lettering is made.
- **Generated artefacts:** an Astro component that turns an item's data into an SVG.
- **Full-width objects in the shell:** the page sits in the shell's main column (capped at `--measure-wide` with a gutter) and the automatic section padding. A ground, a photograph or a band that runs edge to edge takes `class="bleed"` (expressive layer), which leaves the column and gives the gutter back to the content inside; do not widen the main column for the whole page, and do not zero section padding with a selector that outranks each section's own (a trial's flush headings came from `.page > section { padding: 0 }`). Self-hosted fonts follow [day-2 recipes](../../new-site-workflow/references/day-2-recipes.md) (files in `src/styles/fonts/` with `@font-face`, or a Fontsource package).
- **World entrances on scroll:** the object's own motion on a view timeline, inside `@supports (animation-timeline: view())`: `animation-name: <the name in the record>; animation-timing-function: linear; animation-fill-mode: both; animation-timeline: view(); animation-range: entry 0% entry 45%;`, always as longhands: the production minifier folds an `animation` shorthand and an `animation-timeline` of one rule into a value browsers reject, so the motion runs on the dev server and never on the built site (`check:css` fails on it). Items of a list arrive one after another with a growing range end (45%, 53%, 61% …). Browsers without scroll-driven animations show the final state. For the plain text between objects, the starter's `data-reveal` gives the quiet shared entrance.
- **Ambient loops:** an `@keyframes` cycle of 8 seconds or longer with `animation-iteration-count: infinite` on a decorative layer, `animation-play-state: paused` on `:hover` and `:focus-within` of its section.
- **A line that draws itself:** `stroke-dasharray` and `stroke-dashoffset`, on scroll with `animation-timeline: view()` inside `@supports`.
- All motion sits inside `@media (prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference)`.

**When you have named an effect and do not know how to build it, look it up; do not invent it.** For anything driven by the scroll in plain CSS, the demos at [scroll-driven-animations.style](https://scroll-driven-animations.style/) (Apache 2.0) show the working code. For a named effect (a hover reveal, a text effect, a layout change), the open repositories of [Codrops](https://github.com/codrops) (MIT) hold a working demo of most; read the technique and write it again in the site's own CSS and tokens, and add no dependency for it outside the impression tier. For how other sites lay out one kind of section, [Kombai Gallery](https://kombai.com/gallery/web/) is free to browse by section. Take the technique, never the look: the look comes from step 1.

## 5. Check it

- On the rendered home page at 1440px, name the thing every section is, and the picture it comes from. A section you can only call "a heading with a photograph" is a bare section; one per page.
- Cover the logo. The world (its objects, surfaces and marks) should still say whose site it is.
- Put the page beside the pictures in `audit/world/` and the client's photographs. Its ground, its lettering and its objects should be recognisably taken from them. If the page would suit another trade after a change of words, it is the easy version.
- Count the surfaces, named motions and drawn objects: two surfaces, three motions and the objects of the inventory. `audit:composition` prints the most animations running at once on each page and checks that each named motion runs.
- On the served build, run `npm run audit:composition -- --base <origin> --routes <the home page and one page of each kind>` from the site. It reads the record from `AGENTS.md` and exits 1 on its floor findings, which are fixed, not recorded: `bare-page` (three or more of surface, drawn objects, depth and motion missing), `soft-photo` (a wide photograph shown wider than its file), `world-motion` (a motion named in the record that never runs), and for a direction whose record has a `Sources` line `kicker-openings` (a small label above three or more section headings) and `accent-word` (one word of the page heading in italics). The studio sites measured above have none.
- Score the concepts beside the selected references on the [reference card](reference-research.md#3-judge-every-candidate-on-one-card).

## Record it

```markdown
- **World:**
  - Source object: <the thing the site is built like, the picture that shows it, and why it is this client's>
  - Sources: <at least four, separated by semicolons, each written as the file in backticks, a colon, what the picture shows, an arrow and what the site builds from it. At least two are the client's own photographs and at least two are pictures saved by design:world under audit/world/. check:art-direction checks that each file exists>
  - Inventory: <twelve or more, separated by semicolons>
  - Objects: <per page, the object each section is set as>
  - Surfaces: <the two or three materials the world is made of, the grounds built from them and the edges between them>
  - Type: <display with its treatment and the lettering it is taken from; reading face; a third voice only when a picture shows one, with that picture; largest size>
  - Motion: <an arrival on the first screen starts visible: it moves, turns or scales into place and never starts at opacity 0 or clipped shut, which `check:quality` reports as a first screen that opens hidden. On the impression tier (the record's Motion Tier line), also name the pinned scene's motion here, and after the motions say that the scroll is smoothed and which page transition is used; see astro-site-design/references/impression-motion.md. On every tier: three to five motions, each written as its @keyframes name in backticks, a colon and the object it belongs to. Name the page transition, the scroll library and other starter words after them without that form: only a backticked name followed by a colon or a bracket is checked as an animation that must run>
  - Photographs: <the frames and the tone treatment>
  - Feature: <the working feature, the job it does and the material it is built from>
- **Decisions:**
  - Look. Easy: <the sector default>. Ours: <what this site does>
  - Feature. Easy: <...>. Ours: <...>
  - Content. Easy: <...>. Ours: <...>
```
